Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae) is one of the most destructive fungal diseases of rice in India, especially during the Kharif season. It affects leaves, nodes, necks, and panicles. Symptoms include diamond-shaped lesions with gray centers and brown borders on leaves; infected nodes turn black and break. Neck blast causes the panicle to fall over (panicle blight). High humidity, temperatures between 25–28°C, and excessive nitrogen fertilization favor infection. Organic treatment: spray Pseudomonas fluorescens at 2.5 kg/ha dissolved in 500 liters of water. Spray neem oil (3%) at the first sign of infection. Chemical treatment: Tricyclazole 75 WP at 0.6 g/liter or Carbendazim 50 WP at 1 g/liter. Spray at tillering and panicle initiation stages. Preventive measures include resistant varieties (IR-64, Improved Samba Mahsuri), seed treatment with Carbendazim, and balanced nitrogen application.

Tomato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans) is a water mold disease that causes rapid destruction of tomato foliage and fruit in India, particularly in hilly regions and during cool, wet weather. Symptoms include large, irregular, dark-brown or olive-green lesions on leaves with a white downy mold on the underside. Infected stems turn dark brown and collapse. Fruit develops large, firm, brown, greasy-looking lesions. Cool temperatures (15–20°C) combined with high humidity (over 90%) trigger epidemics. Organic treatment: spray copper-based fungicides (Bordeaux mixture 1%) every 7–10 days. Use Trichoderma viride at 5 g/liter as a soil drench. Chemical treatment: Mancozeb 75 WP at 2 g/liter or Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (Ridomil Gold) at 2.5 g/liter. Spray preventively before disease onset. Remove and destroy infected plant material immediately. Avoid overhead irrigation and waterlogging.

Wheat Rust encompasses three types common in India: stem rust (Puccinia graminis), leaf rust (Puccinia triticina), and yellow/stripe rust (Puccinia striiformis). Leaf rust is the most widespread, appearing as small, round, reddish-brown pustules on upper leaf surfaces. Yellow rust forms yellow-orange pustules in linear stripes on leaves and glumes. Stem rust causes large, dark-brown to black pustules on stems and leaves. Rust spreads rapidly via wind-borne spores. Moderate temperatures (15–22°C for yellow rust, 20–25°C for leaf rust) and high humidity favor spread. Organic treatment: early sowing of resistant varieties (HD-2967, PBW-550 for leaf rust). Spray neem-based formulations to suppress early infection. Chemical treatment: Propiconazole 25 EC at 0.1% or Tebuconazole 25 WG at 0.1%. Apply at first appearance of symptoms and repeat after 15 days. Seed treatment with Carboxin + Thiram helps prevent early infection.

Cotton Bollworm (Helicoverpa armigera) is the most economically damaging pest of cotton in India, also attacking tomato, chickpea, sorghum, and maize. The caterpillar (greenish with stripes) bores into bolls, flowers, and green fruits, causing them to drop. Mature larvae are 3.5–4 cm long with a broad pale stripe along the sides. Adults are yellowish-brown moths that lay eggs on young leaves and flower buds. Chemical treatment: spray Emamectin benzoate 5 SG at 0.4 g/liter or Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC at 0.3 ml/liter. Spinosad 45 SC at 0.3 ml/liter is effective for early instars. Biological control: release Trichogramma chilonis egg parasitoids at 1.5 lakh/ha per week during egg-laying period. Install pheromone traps (5/ha) for monitoring and mass trapping. Pick and destroy egg masses and young larvae by hand. Avoid planting susceptible crops in adjoining fields. Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis) spray at 2 g/liter is effective against young larvae.

Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) is an invasive pest that reached India in 2018 and now causes severe damage to maize (corn), sorghum, and sugarcane across multiple states. The caterpillar is identified by an inverted Y-shaped suture on its head and four black spots in a square on the second-to-last abdominal segment. Young larvae feed on leaf surfaces, creating windowing; older larvae bore into whorls causing a distinctive sawdust-like frass. Damage appears as holes in leaves and ragged defoliation. Chemical treatment: Spinetoram 11.7 SC at 0.5 ml/liter or Emamectin benzoate 5 SG at 0.4 g/liter sprayed directly into the whorl. Chlorpyrifos 20 EC mixed with sand (1:10) applied into whorls. Biological control: Bacillus thuringiensis var. kurstaki at 2 g/liter, spray into the whorl in the evening. Release egg parasitoids (Telenomus remus, Trichogramma pretiosum) at egg stage. Apply neem-based products (Azadirachtin 1500 ppm) at 5 ml/liter into whorls. Early detection via pheromone traps (5/ha) with FAW-specific lures is critical.

Red Rot of Sugarcane (Colletotrichum falcatum) is a major fungal disease causing significant yield loss in North Indian sugarcane belts. The disease is called red rot because infected stalks show a characteristic red discoloration inside, often with white patches. External symptoms include yellowing and drying of the third and fourth leaves from the top; canes emit a sour, alcoholic odor when split. The disease spreads through infected seed cane, soil, and waterlogging. Organic treatment: treat seed pieces with Trichoderma viride (4 g/liter) for 30 minutes before planting. Avoid waterlogging by improving field drainage. Chemical treatment: treat setts with Carbendazim 50 WP (1 g/liter) or Thiophanate methyl (1 g/liter) by immersing for 20 minutes. Rogue out and destroy infected clumps immediately. Use resistant varieties (Co-0238 is susceptible; prefer Co-86032, CoLk-94184 where available). Crop rotation with legumes or wheat helps break the disease cycle.

Groundnut Leaf Spot (Cercospora arachidicola and Phaeoisariopsis personata) causes early and late leaf spot diseases respectively, which are the most common foliar diseases of groundnut (peanut) in India. Early leaf spot appears on upper leaf surfaces as roughly circular brown spots with yellow halos; late leaf spot produces darker spots on the lower surface. Both diseases cause premature defoliation, reducing pod filling and yield by 50–70% in severe cases. High humidity and temperatures between 25–30°C favor infection. Organic treatment: apply Bordeaux mixture (1%) at 15-day intervals starting from 30 days after sowing. Neem oil spray (2 ml/liter) suppresses early infection. Chemical treatment: Mancozeb 75 WP at 2.5 g/liter or Chlorothalonil 75 WP at 2 g/liter. Propiconazole 25 EC at 0.5 ml/liter is effective for late leaf spot. Apply fungicides every 10–14 days starting at first symptom appearance. Use resistant or tolerant varieties (ICGV-86031, TAG-24) and avoid excessive irrigation. Collect and destroy fallen infected leaves to reduce inoculum.
