# Soil pH and Soil Health Guide

## Understanding Soil pH
Soil pH measures the acidity or alkalinity of the soil on a scale from 0 to 14.
- Strongly Acidic: pH < 5.5
- Slightly Acidic: pH 5.5 to 6.5
- Neutral: pH 6.5 to 7.5
- Alkaline: pH > 7.5

## Impact of Soil pH on Crops and Nutrient Availability
- Optimal Soil pH Range for most agricultural crops is 6.0 to 7.0.
- Acidic Soils (<5.5): Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, Calcium, and Magnesium become less available to plant roots, while toxic Aluminium and Iron levels increase.
- Alkaline Soils (>7.5): Micronutrients such as Zinc, Iron, Manganese, and Boron become locked and unavailable to plants.

## Crop-Specific Optimal Soil pH Ranges
- Rice: 5.5 to 6.5 (tolerates 5.0 - 7.5)
- Tomato: 6.0 to 6.8
- Wheat: 6.0 to 7.5
- Maize (Corn): 6.0 to 7.2
- Cotton: 6.0 to 8.0
- Sugarcane: 6.0 to 7.5
- Potato: 5.0 to 6.5

## Correcting Soil pH
- Correcting Acidic Soil: Apply Agricultural Lime (Calcium Carbonate) or Dolomitic Lime based on soil test recommendations. Incorporate 1-2 tonnes/ha into topsoil 3-4 weeks before sowing.
- Correcting Alkaline Soil: Apply Gypsum (Calcium Sulphate) or Elemental Sulfur along with organic compost/manure.
