# NPK Deficiencies and Nutrient Management Guide

## 1. Nitrogen (N) Deficiency
- Importance: Nitrogen is essential for vegetative growth, chlorophyll synthesis, and protein formation.
- Symptoms: Uniform yellowing (chlorosis) of older lower leaves first, starting from the leaf tips. Stunted plant growth, thin stems, reduced tillering in cereal crops like rice and wheat.
- Correction: Top dress with Urea (46% N) @ 25-30 kg/acre or Ammonium Sulphate. Foliar spray of 1-2% Urea solution provides quick relief.

## 2. Phosphorus (P) Deficiency
- Importance: Crucial for root development, flowering, seed formation, and energy transfer (ATP).
- Symptoms: Dark green to reddish-purple discoloration on older leaves and stems, stunted root growth, delayed maturity.
- Correction: Apply Single Super Phosphate (SSP - 16% P2O5) or Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP - 46% P2O5, 18% N) as basal application during soil preparation.

## 3. Potassium (K) Deficiency
- Importance: Regulates water balance, stomatal opening, disease resistance, and fruit/grain quality.
- Symptoms: Marginal yellowing and scorching (browning/drying) of older leaf tips and edges, weak stems prone to lodging.
- Correction: Apply Muriate of Potash (MOP - 60% K2O) @ 15-20 kg/acre or Sulfate of Potash (SOP).

## Integrated Nutrient Management (INM)
- Combine inorganic chemical fertilizers with organic manures (FYM 5-10 tonnes/acre), Vermicompost, and bio-fertilizers (Azospirillum, PSB).
