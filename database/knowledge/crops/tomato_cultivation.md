# Tomato Cultivation Guide

## Overview
Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is one of the most widely grown vegetable crops in India, sensitive to extreme temperatures and moisture stress.

## Ideal Soil pH and Growing Conditions
- Ideal Soil pH: 6.0 to 6.8 (slightly acidic to near neutral).
- Soil Type: Well-drained sandy loam to clay loam rich in organic matter.
- Avoid waterlogged soils as tomato plants are highly susceptible to root rot and bacterial wilt.

## Irrigation Schedule and Water Requirement
- Irrigation Frequency: Tomato plants require regular and uniform watering. Irrigate every 2 to 3 days during warm summer months, or every 5 to 7 days during cooler weather.
- Water Quantity: Provide about 1 to 1.5 inches (25-35 mm) of water per week.
- Method: Drip irrigation is strongly recommended. Avoid overhead sprinkler watering to prevent foliar fungal diseases such as Early Blight and Late Blight.
- Critical Stages: Flowering and fruit development are the most critical moisture phases. Irregular watering leads to fruit cracking and Blossom End Rot (caused by localized calcium deficiency).

## Fertilizer and Nutrient Management
- NPK Requirement: 100:60:60 kg/ha.
- Apply full Phosphorus and 50% Potash as basal application.
- Top-dress Nitrogen in 3 split doses at 20, 45, and 65 days after transplanting.
- Foliar spray of Calcium Nitrate (0.5%) at flowering helps prevent Blossom End Rot.
