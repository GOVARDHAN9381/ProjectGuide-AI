# Light & Photoperiod Requirements for Crops

This document summarizes lighting (daylength) and light intensity considerations for common crops, useful for greenhouse/controlled-environment guidance and planting timing.

## Daylength (photoperiod)
- Photoperiod-sensitive crops respond to day length for flowering and development:
  - Short-day crops: e.g., rice varieties (some), prefer shorter daylight to induce flowering.
  - Long-day crops: some temperate crops flower with longer days.

## Light intensity
- Field crops: measured in PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation) or lux; typical outdoor PAR is 1500–2000 µmol·m−2·s−1 at noon; plants adapt to full sun.
- Greenhouse/LED guidance:
  - Rice/maize/tomato: ~300–600 µmol·m−2·s−1 daily average for good growth (depending on variety and stage).
  - Seedlings: lower intensity initially (50–150 µmol·m−2·s−1) with higher hours of light.

## Practical advisory
- For open-field crops, focus on planting windows, not artificial light (unless protected cultivation).
- For greenhouse growers: provide daily light integral (DLI) targets and adjust photoperiod to control flowering.
