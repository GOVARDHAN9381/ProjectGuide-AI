# Agricultural Irrigation Management Guide

## Overview of Irrigation Methods
Proper irrigation scheduling is vital for optimizing crop yield, improving water-use efficiency, and preventing soil degradation.

## Tomato Irrigation Schedule
- Frequency: Water tomato plants every 2 to 3 days during summer, and every 5 to 7 days in winter.
- Best Method: Drip irrigation delivering water directly to the root zone.
- Critical Period: Flowering, fruit set, and fruit enlargement stages require consistent moisture.
- Caution: Over-irrigation causes root rot, while inconsistent moisture leads to fruit cracking and blossom end rot.

## Rice Irrigation Management
- Water Requirement: High water requirement (1000 to 1400 mm).
- Tillering Stage: Maintain 2 to 5 cm standing water level.
- Panicle Initiation to Flowering: Keep field submerged under 5 cm water.
- Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD): Allow standing water to naturally subside to 15 cm below soil surface before re-irrigating. Saves up to 30% water without reducing yield.

## Soil Moisture Indicators
- Sandy Soils: Fast drainage, requires frequent light irrigations.
- Clay Soils: High water retention, requires infrequent heavy irrigations.
- Squeeze Test: Squeeze a handful of soil from root depth; if it forms a ball without crumbling, moisture is adequate.
